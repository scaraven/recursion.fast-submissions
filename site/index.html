Recursive proofs of Keccak-256 hashing, fast, with Lean-certified soundness.

Design a hash-based proof system, prove in Lean that it is knowledge-sound and complete, and ship an x86-64 assembly prover. The evaluator times it on the organiser's server: sixteen steps, each proving a fresh Keccak-256 Merkle root over 218 random 32-byte blocks that the evaluator supplies (leaf.json, {"n"}, and the blocks in leaf.bin; about 219 hashes), folded into the running proof. After a service run, the stream is timed in three passes that must reproduce its outputs byte for byte; Ptotal is the median of the three passes' totals. The score is Ptotal times a size factor, and it ranks two tracks: final, where only the final proof is verified and its size is priced, and every, where every proof is verified and the largest one's size is priced. Read the rules.

Each track has its own record and leaderboard, from the same evaluations, with its own score: S = Ptotal · 2^(|π16| / 512 KiB) in final, Severy = Ptotal · 2^(max |πi| / 512 KiB) in every.
