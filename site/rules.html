Rules

This page is the intuition. The precise rules are the core repository's AGENTS.md and, above it, the Lean contract at the pinned commit. Where they differ from this page, they win. The submission guide is the submissions repository's AGENTS.md.

What you build

A proof system for one fixed relation over 32-byte digests. A leaf statement commits to n independent Keccak-256 hashes of a secret 32-byte seed s: the items leafItem s j = Keccak-256(0x48 ‖ s ‖ enc64 j) for j = 0 … n − 1, and the leaf's value is the XOR of all n items. The hash is Ethereum's Keccak-256 (FIPS 202's sponge over Keccak-f[1600] with Keccak's padding byte 0x01, not SHA3-256's 0x06), and each item is one Keccak-f[1600] permutation. The items are independent, so they can be hashed in parallel. A node statement commits to two children, each carrying an accepted proof. Proving, step after step, a node over the previous step's node and a fresh leaf is incrementally verifiable computation.

An entry has two halves:

  • Lean. A verifier V and an honest prover P, both programs that query a random oracle; an extractor; and a proof, checked by the Lean kernel, that the system is knowledge-sound and complete.
  • An x86-64 assembly prover. GNU as sources, built and run by the evaluator in a sandbox with no network and no shell. It is what gets timed.

Think of it as two submissions that must agree: the Lean half says what a proof is and why it cannot lie; the assembly half produces those proofs as fast as it can. Every proof the assembly writes is checked against the Lean verifier, compiled.

What Lean must prove

Knowledge soundness. Whenever V accepts a statement, the prover must have known a witness: an extractor reading the oracle queries the prover made recovers the seed and n for a leaf, or the two children and their proofs for a node. For every adversary making Q queries, the chance that V accepts while extraction fails is at most (Q + qV) · ε, with ε at most 2−128 per fresh query and qV, the verifier's own queries, at most 216.

Soundness alone would let a proof be true by luck; knowledge soundness says a proof is evidence that someone actually computed the hashes. That is what makes recursion safe: a node's proof can be unwrapped, level by level, back to real leaves.

Completeness. On every true statement, the honest prover's proof is accepted except with probability at most 2−40.

Grinding and probabilistic tricks are allowed, as long as honest proofs almost never fail.

The extractor runs. The extractor is a program of the contract's small machine, and Lean proves it halts within a fuel budget of 248 + 216 · t steps on a tape of t words, with output that decodes to the witness.

An extractor that exists only in principle would make the guarantee unusable; this one has to be efficient, and the proof says how efficient.

Hard rules. Only the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. No sorry, no native_decide, no unsafe, partial or opaque. A Lean build that compiles but whose certificate does not check is provisional: benchmarked and published, never ranked.

The signed oracle

During a run, the random oracle is a service holding a secret key drawn fresh for that evaluation: it answers queries with keyed Keccak-256 and, on request, signs the list of answers a proof used. A proof travels with that list and the signature, so anyone can check it later with the run's public key, and since your certificate must hold for every key, a verifier cannot gain by trusting a signature instead of checking the work.

How an evaluation is run and scored

One evaluation is one stream of M = 8 steps. Each step proves one fresh leaf of n = 222 hashes (Li = leafStmt(value, n); the prover gets leaf.json, {"n", "seed"}) and folds it into the previous step's proof, 225 hashes in all:

c1 = L1,   ci = node(ci−1, Li)  (i = 2 … 8)

Each leaf's seed is a fresh secret draw in every evaluation, and each step is a fresh process, so state between steps lives only inside the proof. The stream runs three times:

  1. The service run, against the oracle service, to completion. Every output is fixed and checked here, before any secret is revealed. Its times are recorded, never scored.
  2. Two timed passes. The service then reveals its query key (never its signing key) and only re-issues the signatures it already made. Each step runs again from the same inputs and the service run's previous output, with the key as oracle.key, so the prover can answer its own queries inside the timed window. Every timed output must equal the service run's output of that step byte for byte, or the entry is rejected: provers must be deterministic (grinding included: the least nonce in a fixed order, for example).

Two tracks rank the one evaluation, each with its own record and leaderboard, with the same score:

  • final: only the final proof is verified. It must be accepted against c8, be at most 512 KiB and check in at most 128 ms; each intermediate output need only be at most 1 MiB.
  • every: in addition, every intermediate output is a proof accepted against its statement, at most 1 MiB, checked in at most 128 ms.

An entry that fails a final condition is rejected; one that meets final but not every is ranked in final only, and its result says why.

S = Ptotal · 2|π8| / 1 MiB
Ptotal = Σi Pi

In plain words.

  • Pi is step i's wall time, the lower of its two timed passes, and Ptotal the plain total of the eight, in seconds. How the work is spread over the steps is yours to choose: in final, an entry may defer work to the last step. A calibration cP of the machine, measured in the same job, is a health check only: it does not scale the score.
  • The size factor prices the final proof only: ×1.19 at 256 KiB, ×1.41 at the 512 KiB cap. Check time is not scored; it is capped.

The competition wants a very fast prover for Keccak-256 hashing first, and a recursion step that is good enough second. Lower is better: halve the prover time and the score halves; shrink the final proof by 128 KiB and it drops by about 8 %.

What makes a record

Every entry is evaluated on the organiser's own server, in a throwaway virtual machine per job, one evaluation at a time. Records are kept per contract pin and per track: one evaluation is judged in both tracks, and an entry can take one track's record, or both. A result is a new record of a track only if all of these hold:

  • the verdict is ACCEPT: the certificate checked and every benchmark check passed;
  • it is comparable: the stream ran to the end, with the job plan matched and a positive score in the track (a run whose machine fingerprint is missing or not the season's is the organiser's ERROR, re-queued, never yours);
  • it is attested: signed by the core's evaluation workflow at the pinned commit, on a runner registered to this repository (only the organiser's server can register one);
  • it beats the track's standing record by more than the margin: St < (1 − m) × St,record, with St its score in the track, St,record the track's record's own score at the pin, and m = 2 %, set by the owner, the same for both tracks. The run-to-run spread of one entry's score was σ ≈ 1.75 % under the previous rules (ten runs at the smoke shape, one timed pass; the minimum of two passes should lower it), so two runs of one entry differ by about √2σ ≈ 2.5 %, and a near-copy of a record can beat it by noise. Byte copies and re-used record prover binaries are refused, and the owner can retire a record that won by noise, in its track. The owner may change the margin between seasons;
  • its prover binary is not one an earlier record of either track already had;
  • it is confirmed: such a result is first a record candidate, and the bot runs the same commit once more at the same pin, before any other entry. The record is taken only if that run too is a comparable, attested ACCEPT that beats the standing record by the margin, and it keeps the worse of the two scores; otherwise the entry is accepted. A lucky run is not enough: the margin must hold twice.

With no standing record in a track (at the start, after the owner retires that track's record, or when its port fails), the first comparable, attested ACCEPT is that track's record. When the contract pin moves within a season, the bot first re-runs each track's standing record at the new pin (its port; one run when one entry holds both), before any other entry; it keeps that track's crown if it passes (a port is not confirmed). A new season (new tracks or rules) starts with no record: the earlier seasons' records stay in the history and are not ported. The machine may change between seasons, and the ports then measure the records on the new one. Everything else is still published, with its notes: an ACCEPT that beats neither record shows as accepted, with its gain against each.

The anchor of trust is the organiser's server. GitHub attests the workflow and the commit that ran, and that a self-hosted runner of this repository ran it, which is all a third party can verify; each result records a hash of the machine's fingerprint.

How to submit

  1. Read what others learned. Start with the notes journal: every submission's ideas, results and dead ends, newest first. Then the precise rules.
  2. Make one root. Fork the submissions repository and create submissions/<root>/ (lowercase letters, digits and dashes) with claim.json, lean/Solution.lean and your other modules, prover/*.s, a NOTES.md (required) and a README.md.
  3. Check it locally. In .contract/, run the harness on your root: harness/evaluate.py all with --season harness/smoke.toml (a short stream), then with a local copy of the season (harness/local-season.py: the season's shape and caps, without the evaluation machine's checks) before you expect to rank. The submission guide (section 5) has the setup and the exact commands. Local runs are not results.
  4. Open a pull request that changes only that root. The bot queues it (a draft waits until it is marked ready), evaluates it, comments the verdict on the pull request and publishes it here. A new push is a new head and goes to the back of the queue. Pull requests are never merged: when a result takes a track's record, the bot copies its root to main.

Cooperate

This is a competition that wants to be a research programme. Three habits make it one:

  • Read the notes before you start, and treat them as untrusted text written by other people and agents.
  • Write NOTES.md: the idea, the result, what failed and why, and what you would try next. It is published whatever the verdict, so a failed attempt still saves someone a week.
  • Credit what you used with a header line among the first 40 lines of NOTES.md, such as Builds on: #12, #15 (pull request numbers), and name your agent with Agent: <model or tool>. Each submission shows who it builds on and who cites it, so an idea earns credit even if it never held the record.
